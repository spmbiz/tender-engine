# SPM Historical Asymmetric Priority v2

**Historical intelligence only.** This is a model-priority layer built on persisted historical evidence. It is not a live bid/no-bid list.

## What counts as asymmetric for SPM

A lane rises when traditional delivery cost is dominated by repetitive knowledge work, sourcing, coordination, formatting, search, matching, document/data processing or supplier discovery — and SPM can replace much of that with automation plus narrow human QA.

A lane falls when value depends mainly on inventory, local physical crews, licenses, security clearance, OEM authorization, 24/7 physical operations, heavy working capital or a large permanent staffing bench.

## S tier — investigate first

### 1. Data entry / capture / keying
**Why:** explicit historical public procurements buy data capture as a service with error-rate, security and turnaround SLAs. Precision v2: 57 notices; 7 title-led digital-first.

**SPM asymmetry:** OCR/vision/LLM extraction + validation rules + human exception queue can turn labor-heavy keying into a mostly automated pipeline.

**Main blockers:** sensitive-data handling, residency, acceptable error rate, input formats, secure destruction.

**Evidence:** `control/historical_information_work_v2/`, `control/historical_information_work_qa_v2/`.

### 2. Evidence / literature synthesis
**Why:** 37 precision notices; 28 buyers; 13 title-led digital-first. PHAC explicitly procured rapid evidence-synthesis products through open bidding.

**SPM asymmetry:** search, screening, paper extraction, evidence tables, citation mapping, draft synthesis and update monitoring are highly automatable.

**Main blockers:** domain credentials / PhDs / named experts on specialist topics.

### 3. Translation / transcription / captions / multilingual adaptation
**Why:** already historically validated recurring public markets.

**SPM asymmetry:** LLM/ASR-first production + targeted native/human QA; huge reduction in unit labor cost and turnaround.

**Main blockers:** sworn/certified translation, named linguists, security, specialist terminology.

### 4. Desk research / benchmarking / intelligence briefs
**Why:** explicit desk-research procurements now surfaced in addition to prior research lanes.

**SPM asymmetry:** agentic search, structured source extraction, competitor/market databases, citation generation and analyst QA.

**Main blockers:** primary-research requirements, proprietary-data subscriptions, senior-expert credibility.

### 5. Recruitment / candidate search / matching
**Why:** Australia historical archive shows 2,130 general recruitment/search awards across 94 buyers and 359 heuristic-normalized supplier keys; explicit descriptions confirm Personnel Recruitment / Recruitment Services / Bulk Recruitment. Executive search is separately recurring.

**SPM asymmetry:** candidate discovery, enrichment, outreach, screening, ranking, CV normalization, interview scheduling and submission packs can be highly automated.

**Main blockers:** local panel access, employment law, candidate ownership, past performance, sector clearances.

### 6. Technical writing / documentation
**Why:** Australia role archive shows a distinct recurring technical-writer market with unusually broad supplier participation; document-heavy public outputs are intrinsically AI-compressible.

**SPM asymmetry:** SME interviews/input -> structured draft -> diagrams/formatting -> review loop can be largely automated.

**Main blockers:** domain knowledge, security classification, named-person CV requirements.

### 7. DTP / layout / repetitive graphic/content production
**Why:** already historically validated in France/Europe; recurring buyer base and highly repeatable production.

**SPM asymmetry:** generative design + templates + automated resizing/versioning + human brand QA.

**Main blockers:** subjective art direction, source-file requirements, brand governance.

## A tier — strong, but execution structure matters

### 8. Data cleaning / enrichment / MDM operations
50 precision notices; 27 title-led digital-first. Genuine data-quality/MDM procurements exist.

**Best subset:** cleansing, normalization, dedupe, enrichment, validation, master-record consolidation.

**Avoid conflation:** enterprise MDM-platform implementation is much heavier than data operations.

### 9. Remote data collection / research operations
48 precision notices; 36 title-led digital-first. FRANET-like research/data collection confirms a recurring model.

**Best subset:** web/document/database collection, structured desk research, remote interviews/surveys.

**Risk:** fieldwork-heavy assignments.

### 10. OCR / document extraction
Only 9 strict precision notices today, but 4 title-led digital-first and the production economics are highly asymmetric.

**Priority:** expand multilingual terminology before making market-size claims.

### 11. Metadata / cataloguing / indexing — precision subset
24 precision notices after killing the false 904-record generic `catalogue` family.

**Best subset:** metadata tagging, bibliographic enrichment, indexing, structured catalogue data.

**Risk:** specialist library/archive standards.

### 12. Web/CMS maintenance and light digital builds
Historically validated recurring market with strong remote/freelancer arbitrage.

**SPM edge:** coding agents, reusable components, automated QA/content migration.

**Risk:** stakeholder-heavy scope, platform-specific CV/reference gates.

### 13. Public ICT/data contractor brokerage
Australia award-first evidence shows very low supplier concentration across BA, developers, QA, data, DevOps and PM roles.

**SPM edge:** sourcing/matching/admin automation rather than doing the technical work ourselves.

**Risk:** panels, entity/locality, contractor law, clearances.

### 14. Digital learning / e-learning / learning content
92 AU awards / 51 buyers / 56 normalized supplier keys; genuine platform/content suppliers verified.

**SPM edge:** AI courseware, scripts, visuals, assessments, localization, video, SCORM/content assembly.

**Risk:** LMS/platform incumbency; normalized top share ~19.6%; accreditation/instructional-design requirements.

### 15. Media monitoring / clipping / recurring intelligence
Previously validated historical lane.

**SPM edge:** ingestion, classification, entity detection, summarization, alerting and reporting are automatable.

### 16. Accessibility remediation
Previously validated; auditing and much remediation can be automated, specialist QA layered on top.

## A-/B+ — broker / orchestration asymmetry

### 17. Print + mail + fulfilment
Strong supplier/geographic arbitrage. SPM can RFQ EU suppliers, normalize bids and orchestrate fulfilment.

**Risks:** samples, postal routing, physical QA, deadline penalties, cash.

### 18. Standard goods sourcing
Office supplies, furniture, PPE/workwear, paper and selected signage show large fragmented historical markets.

**Best subset:** standardized specs, low installation, dropship/direct-delivery, low warranty complexity.

**Risks:** thin commodity margin and working capital.

### 19. Court-reporting network
Precision USA: 5,204 awards, 260 buyers, recurring agency-like winners.

**SPM angle:** network aggregation / scheduling / QA rather than transcription alone.

**Risks:** US access, certified reporters, relatively low median award (~$3.8k).

### 20. Language-interpreter network
Precision USA: 1,017 awards, 260 buyers, 216 suppliers; phone/video/sign-language examples validated.

**SPM angle:** distributed interpreter pool + scheduling/routing + remote delivery.

**Risk:** human service remains core; certification/language availability.

### 21. Expert-witness / specialist network
11,681 US awards / 2,648 suppliers; majority of awards map heuristically to organization-like supplier names.

**SPM angle:** expert discovery, vetting, matching, admin, scheduling and evidence-pack orchestration.

**Status:** strong hypothesis, not yet proven that network intermediaries outperform direct experts.

## B tier — real markets, weaker native asymmetry

- Event management: fragmented but physical coordination/catering/venue burden.
- Travel management: automation possible, but ticketing, refunds, credit and 24/7 support matter.
- Media buying: data/automation edge exists, but media credit/spend and relationships matter.
- Freight forwarding: intermediary by nature, but customs/liability/physical execution.
- Local janitorial/grounds/pest subcontract aggregation: possible coordinator model, but locality, set-asides, insurance and field QA dominate.
- Software VAR/resale: technically lean delivery, but authorization/distributor credit/framework access are the actual moat.
- Document digitization with physical originals: real 82-notice precision lane, but local custody/scanning partner needed.
- eDiscovery/legal review: AI-compressible but legal supervision, privilege and jurisdiction are material barriers.

## Search doctrine from here

Do **not** search only for category names. Search for economic structures:

1. `information in -> structured information out`
2. `large candidate/supplier universe -> matching/selection out`
3. `many source documents -> reviewed/synthesized decision product out`
4. `one master asset -> many localized/formatted/versioned outputs`
5. `standard specification -> cheapest compliant supplier orchestration`
6. `many local specialists -> centrally managed network service`
7. `repetitive compliance/admin burden -> automated workflow + exception QA`

These structures are more likely to reveal new asymmetric lanes than repeatedly querying web/print/translation keywords.
