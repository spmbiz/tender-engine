## Tier B+ — worth dedicated economics / margin tests

- **Office consumables / toner resale — BROKER/RESELL.** Multiple historical clusters are standardized and fragmented enough to investigate. Avoid highly concentrated cohorts. Deep-dive OEM/original-toner constraints, supplier credit and delivery economics.
- **Pure data/content migration — CORE/PARTNER.** v3.1 leaves 11 clean pure-service rows after stripping large IT implementations. Smaller market than v2 implied, but real.
- **Print/mail/fulfilment brokerage — BROKER.** Existing historical evidence plus EU supplier-arbitrage thesis remains strong where contract value and delivery terms support margin.
- **Promotional merchandise / standardized goods — BROKER.** Existing promoted broker lane; needs SKU/margin/cash discipline.

## Tier B — structurally interesting but do not rush

- **Expert witness network — NETWORK/PARTNER:** 7,674 strict US awards / 2,088 suppliers; repeat firms exist, but direct experts are material and network economics are not yet proven dominant.
- **Australia project/program assurance — PARTNER:** 607 awards /115 suppliers but only 19 buyers; many reviews appear specialist/panel/seniority dependent.
- **Data cleaning/enrichment — CORE/PARTNER:** only 7 clean pure-service rows after platform/MDM implementations are removed; attractive mechanism but historical pure corpus is thin.
- **OCR/text extraction pure service — CORE:** only 3 strict pure-service rows after software and physical scanning are separated; broader digitization business remains real but different.
- **Local-service aggregation — NETWORK:** janitorial/pest/grounds/laundry are real but physical QA, locality, insurance and margin leakage weaken the asymmetry.

## Hold / reclassify

- **Technical Test & Evaluation — Australia:** HARD/PARTNER; 67 rows isolated from false policy-evaluation bucket, concentrated in 2 buyers and specialist/Defence work.
- **Generic evaluation — Australia:** 168 rows remain unresolved; do not promote until semantic decomposition.
- **Food supply brokerage:** high logistics/perishability/margin friction.
- **Laboratory reagents:** compatibility/regulatory/approved-product friction; only revisit non-regulated generic consumables separately.
- **Furniture with installation:** potentially brokerable but local install/logistics and high incumbent concentration often dominate.
- **Electricity/energy aggregation:** potentially intermediary-shaped but market/regulatory complexity is not a lean default.

## Current asymmetric business archetypes

1. **Information in → structured output out** — data entry, validation, synthesis, research, migration.
2. **Demand in → right human out** — recruitment, court reporters, interpreters, reviewers, specialist networks.
3. **Specification in → sourced SKU out** — toner, office/IT consumables, print, promo goods, standardized goods.
4. **Buyer need in → orchestrated specialist deliverable out** — independent reviews, policy evaluations, expert/assurance work.

These archetypes are more useful than a fixed ontology because they describe *why* SPM can have a cost or coordination advantage.
