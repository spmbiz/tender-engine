# SPM Historical Asymmetry Scorecard v1

**Status:** MODEL_HYPOTHESIS layer. Historical evidence-backed where cited by repo path, but the asymmetry scores are GPT strategic judgments, not source facts.

Purpose: separate **historical market attractiveness** from **SPM-specific asymmetric advantage**.

## Scoring dimensions

Each 1–5:

- **AI compression** — how much traditional labor cost/time can plausibly be compressed by AI/automation.
- **Sourcing arbitrage** — ability to source talent/suppliers globally or across lower-cost EU markets.
- **Remote / low-capex** — ability to deliver without facilities, inventory, fleet or onsite workforce.
- **Standardization** — repeatable inputs/outputs, reusable workflows, catalog-like delivery.
- **Fragmentation edge** — historical breadth of buyers/suppliers suggests room beyond a few locked incumbents.
- **Barrier friendliness** — 5 = low apparent channel/regulatory/panel/certification burden; 1 = likely high.
- **Cash friendliness** — 5 = little inventory/media/travel/working-capital exposure; 1 = heavy pre-financing likely.

`ASYM` is directional, not a mathematical truth. It exists to decide what to investigate deeper.

## Core strategic read

The strongest asymmetric pattern is **not simply “digital.”** It is:

> **high-value public outputs whose traditional supplier economics are dominated by repetitive knowledge-work, coordination, sourcing, document production or information processing — while the buyer still prices them as human-service contracts.**

That means future historical mining should actively search for:

1. labor-heavy document workflows;
2. repetitive research/monitoring/administrative workflows;
3. data conversion / migration / cataloguing / tagging / validation;
4. content localization and multi-format adaptation;
5. remote QA/testing/review work;
6. supplier/talent aggregation where discovery and matching are the core service;
7. standardized products with fragmented suppliers and low installation burden;
8. framework categories where a small coordinator can orchestrate specialist subcontractors.

## Negative asymmetry flags

A historically attractive lane should be demoted when the business model requires one or more of:

- expensive inventory or pre-financing;
- mandatory onsite teams across many locations;
- regulated/licensed professionals as core delivery;
- security clearances that cannot be subcontracted around;
- OEM authorization controlled by a few vendors;
- heavy installation/maintenance obligations;
- 24/7 physical operations;
- highly concentrated incumbent supplier structure;
- large permanent staffing bench before award.

None of these flags delete or reject the historical market. They only alter SPM-specific strategic priority.
