# Historical Intelligence — Model Hypotheses

These are strategic interpretations, not source facts. They are deliberately versioned so later evidence can confirm, weaken or reverse them without losing prior reasoning.

## H-001 — Public contractor brokerage may be a larger SPM opportunity than classic digital agency work

**Status:** ACTIVE / HIGH PRIORITY

Evidence basis: AusTender award-first role decomposition shows many recurring ICT/digital contractor roles with broad supplier participation and low top-supplier shares. Business Analyst, Software Developer/Engineer, Data Analyst, Data Engineer, QA, DevOps, Technical Writer, Project/Program Management and Agile/Delivery all appear as distinct recurring markets.

Why asymmetric for SPM: candidate sourcing, screening, CV normalization, multilingual outreach, matching, proposal assembly and ongoing pipeline intelligence are unusually automatable. Delivery labor can be sourced externally rather than built as permanent headcount.

Unknowns/barriers: panel access, local entity, employment/contractor law, security clearance, insurance, candidate-reference requirements, cash-flow terms, onshore requirements.

Next tests: buyer recurrence by role; winner archetypes; SME vs large staffing firms; contract duration; panel/framework prevalence; inferred gross-margin bands from public rate cards where available.

## H-002 — Standardized goods are attractive when specification complexity is low and supplier sourcing is geographically broad

**Status:** ACTIVE / HIGH PRIORITY

Evidence basis: title-led historical cohorts show recurring office supplies, office furniture, PPE/workwear, paper/envelopes, school/art supplies, signage and selected standardized goods across many buyers.

Asymmetry: SPM can use automated RFQ sourcing, EU-wide supplier comparison, catalog normalization, price scraping/quotes, logistics orchestration and bid automation. The edge is strongest when incumbent advantage is low, spec is standardized, installation/after-sales is minimal and working-capital burden can be managed.

Risk: physical logistics, samples, local delivery SLAs, stock commitments, warranty, framework rebates, credit terms and commodity price volatility can erase apparent margin.

## H-003 — Document/content/data operations are unusually AI-compressible

**Status:** ACTIVE / VERY HIGH PRIORITY

Candidate families: translation, transcription, proofreading, DTP/layout, OCR/digitization, data cleaning/enrichment, media monitoring, content adaptation, accessibility remediation, document production and selected research outputs.

Asymmetry: AI can collapse variable labor cost while human QA can be concentrated on edge cases. These markets are often remote, repeatable, language-heavy and administratively burdensome for traditional suppliers.

Need to distinguish: contracts requiring named linguists/CVs, security clearance, sworn translation, native-speaker certifications, sensitive-data hosting, physical document handling or bespoke onsite research.

## H-004 — Software licence/cloud resale can be excellent only where channel authorization is obtainable

**Status:** ACTIVE / CONDITIONAL

Historical evidence supports explicit softwarebroker/VAR/reseller procurements in several countries. The market is real. However, the edge depends less on software fulfillment and more on vendor authorization, credit terms, distributor relationships, public-sector pricing tiers and framework access.

SPM asymmetry may come from aggregating many niche vendors rather than competing head-on in highly concentrated Microsoft/Adobe resale.

## H-005 — Travel management and media buying are real intermediary markets but may require operating infrastructure rather than simple brokering

**Status:** ACTIVE / MEDIUM PRIORITY

Both appear as semantically clean historical agency markets. Potential advantages include automation, booking/ad-tech APIs, low fixed production cost and subcontractable specialist execution. But credit exposure, 24/7 support, ticketing accreditations, media-credit terms, rebate economics and liability may be material.

## H-006 — US administrative/procurement support is a huge market, but award-first fragmentation must not be confused with easy access

**Status:** ACTIVE / MEDIUM PRIORITY

Historical USAspending analysis supports broad supplier participation in office administration, acquisition/procurement support, program support and IT support. The SPM angle is process automation + low-cost analyst/admin labor + AI drafting/research.

Unknowns: set-asides, past-performance, labor categories, clearances, US entity requirements, FAR clauses, incumbent capture and contract vehicles.

## H-007 — The best asymmetric niches may be boring, repetitive, document-heavy and below the attention threshold of large incumbents

**Status:** ACTIVE / CORE SEARCH PRINCIPLE

Search preference: recurring jobs with standardized inputs/outputs, many fragmented buyers, many suppliers, administrative complexity, repeat volume, remote delivery, low regulatory burden and low capex. Avoid equating high contract value with attractiveness.

## H-008 — Open-world discovery must remain independent of the current 90-lane ontology

**Status:** ACTIVE / CORE SEARCH PRINCIPLE

The Atlas is a map, not a filter. Unclassified and misclassified records remain eligible for discovery. Future miners must always reserve an ontology-free lane so new business models can emerge rather than being forced into web/print/software/staffing buckets.
